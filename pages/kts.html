A rare congenital overgrowth syndrome involving vascular malformations and limb hypertrophy

Overview

Klippel-Trenaunay Syndrome (KTS) is a rare congenital vascular disorder characterized by a triad of symptoms: capillary malformations (often appearing as port-wine stains), venous malformations (varicose veins), and limb hypertrophy (overgrowth of bones and soft tissues). It can affect one or more limbs and may involve complications like bleeding, clotting, or lymphatic issues. Diagnosis typically involves imaging and clinical evaluation.

Symptoms

  • Port-wine stains (flat, pink-to-red birthmarks) on the skin
  • Swelling and overgrowth of the affected limb
  • Pain or discomfort in the affected area
  • Varicose veins and venous insufficiency
  • Increased risk of blood clots (deep vein thrombosis)
  • Lymphedema (lymphatic fluid buildup)

Causes

KTS is caused by somatic (non-inherited) mutations in the PIK3CA gene. This mutation occurs randomly during fetal development and is not passed from parent to child. The PIK3CA gene provides instructions for making a protein involved in cell growth and division.

Diagnosis

  • Physical examination of visible vascular malformations and limb asymmetry
  • Ultrasound to assess blood flow and vascular structures
  • MRI for detailed imaging of soft tissue and vascular abnormalities
  • CT scans for bone overgrowth assessment
  • Genetic testing to confirm PIK3CA mutation

Treatment

  • Compression therapy: Graduated compression garments to manage swelling
  • Pain management: Medications and physical therapy for chronic pain
  • Sclerotherapy: Injections to shrink abnormal blood vessels
  • Laser therapy: For port-wine stains and superficial lesions
  • Surgery: Debulking procedures for severe tissue overgrowth
  • Anticoagulation: Blood thinners to reduce clot risk
