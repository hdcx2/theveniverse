Overview

Ehlers-Danlos Syndrome (EDS) is a group of 13 heritable disorders that affect connective tissues, primarily the skin, joints, and blood vessel walls. The most common type is Hypermobile EDS (hEDS).

Symptoms

  • Hypermobile (overly flexible) joints prone to dislocations
  • Skin that is stretchy, fragile, and bruises easily
  • Chronic widespread pain and fatigue
  • Slow wound healing and abnormal scarring
  • Cardiovascular complications (especially vascular EDS)
  • Digestive issues and autonomic dysfunction

Causes

Caused by mutations in collagen genes: COL5A1/COL5A2 (Classical), COL3A1 (Vascular), and others. Most types follow autosomal dominant inheritance. The genetic basis of Hypermobile EDS is still being researched.

Diagnosis

  • Clinical evaluation using the 2017 international criteria
  • Beighton score for joint hypermobility (0-9 scale)
  • Family history and inheritance pattern assessment
  • Genetic testing (confirms most types except hEDS)
  • Skin biopsy in some cases
  • Echocardiogram for cardiovascular screening

Treatment

  • Physical therapy: Muscle strengthening to stabilize joints
  • Pain management: Medications, TENS, CBT
  • Joint protection: Bracing, activity modification
  • Cardiovascular monitoring: Regular imaging for vascular EDS
  • Skin care: Wound care strategies, sun protection
  • Occupational therapy: Adaptive tools for daily living
