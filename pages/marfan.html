Overview

Marfan Syndrome is a genetic disorder that affects the body's connective tissue. It can impact the skeletal system, cardiovascular system, and eyes. People with Marfan Syndrome are often tall and slender with disproportionately long limbs.

Symptoms

  • Tall, slender build with long arms, legs, fingers, and toes
  • Flexible joints and flat feet
  • Heart problems, including aortic dilation and mitral valve prolapse
  • Eye lens dislocation (ectopia lentis)
  • Scoliosis or abnormal spinal curvature
  • Stretch marks unrelated to weight change

Causes

Caused by mutations in the FBN1 gene (fibrillin-1). It follows an autosomal dominant inheritance pattern. About 25% of cases arise from new mutations with no family history.

Diagnosis

  • Physical examination assessing skeletal features
  • Echocardiogram to evaluate aortic root and heart valves
  • Eye examination for lens dislocation
  • Ghent nosology criteria
  • Family history assessment
  • Genetic testing for FBN1 mutations

Treatment

  • Cardiovascular monitoring: Regular echocardiograms
  • Medications: Beta-blockers or ARBs to slow aortic dilation
  • Surgery: Aortic root replacement if needed
  • Vision correction: Glasses, contacts, or surgery
  • Orthopedic care: Bracing or surgery for scoliosis
  • Activity guidance: Low-impact exercise recommended
