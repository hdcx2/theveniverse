Lymphatic Malformations

A reference to common lymphatic malformation patterns, organized by cyst size and body site.

Type: Slow-flow lymphatic anomaly Patterns: Macrocystic, microcystic, or mixed Model files: Add CAD/3D files to this condition when available

Conditions and presentations

Cystic Hygroma (Macrocystic Lymphatic Malformation)

Definition
A group of larger lymph-filled cystic spaces, often found in the neck or axilla.
Causes / pathophysiology
Congenital abnormal development of lymphatic channels; some cases occur with chromosomal or genetic syndromes.
Symptoms / key features
Soft swelling; a large neck lesion can affect swallowing or breathing.
Complications
Infection, bleeding into a lesion, or airway compromise depending on size and location.
Diagnosis
Prenatal or postnatal ultrasound and MRI, selected according to presentation.
Treatment
Specialist management may include image-guided sclerotherapy, surgery, or airway support when needed.
Prognosis
Varies with extent and location; many lesions can be managed, but repeat treatment may be needed.
References
Wassef M et al. “Vascular Anomalies Classification: Recommendations From the International Society for the Study of Vascular Anomalies.” Pediatrics. 2015;136(1):e203–e214.; Burrows PE, Mason KP. “Percutaneous treatment of low flow vascular malformations.” J Vasc Interv Radiol. 2004;15(5):431–445.

Tongue Lymphatic Malformation (Microcystic)

Definition
A microcystic lymphatic malformation involving the tongue, sometimes with a pebbled or vesicular surface.
Causes / pathophysiology
Congenital abnormal development of lymphatic channels.
Symptoms / key features
Tongue swelling, drooling, or difficulty with feeding, speech, and oral care.
Complications
Bleeding, infection, airway effects, or functional difficulty, depending on extent.
Diagnosis
Clinical examination and MRI to assess extent and deeper involvement.
Treatment
May include sclerotherapy, laser procedures, or surgery; repeat treatment is sometimes needed.
Prognosis
Often requires long-term follow-up; symptoms may recur or need more than one treatment.
References
Perkins JA et al. “Lymphatic malformations: current cellular and clinical investigations.” Otolaryngol Head Neck Surg. 2010;142(6):789–794.; Perkins JA et al. “Lymphatic malformations: review of current treatment.” Otolaryngol Head Neck Surg. 2010;142(6):795–803.e1.

Axillary Lymphatic Malformation

Definition
A lymphatic malformation in the armpit and nearby soft tissues.
Causes / pathophysiology
Developmental anomaly of lymphatic channels.
Symptoms / key features
Soft swelling, discomfort, or restricted shoulder or arm movement.
Complications
Infection, bleeding into the lesion, or functional limitation.
Diagnosis
Ultrasound and MRI help characterize the lesion and its extent.
Treatment
Observation, sclerotherapy, or surgery may be considered by a specialist team.
Prognosis
Often favorable, though residual or recurrent disease can require follow-up.
References
Burrows PE, Mason KP. “Percutaneous treatment of low flow vascular malformations.” J Vasc Interv Radiol. 2004;15(5):431–445..

Retroperitoneal Lymphatic Malformation

Definition
A cystic lymphatic lesion in the retroperitoneal space of the abdomen.
Causes / pathophysiology
Developmental anomaly of lymphatic channels.
Symptoms / key features
May cause abdominal fullness, pain, or be found incidentally on imaging.
Complications
Mass effect, infection, bleeding, or bowel and urinary tract compression depending on size and location.
Diagnosis
MRI or CT is used to define location and relationships to nearby structures.
Treatment
Observation, sclerotherapy, or surgery may be considered based on symptoms and anatomy.
Prognosis
Depends on extent and whether complete treatment is feasible; follow-up may be needed.
References
Elluru RG et al. “Lymphatic malformations: diagnosis and management.” Semin Pediatr Surg. 2014;23(4):178–185. doi:10.1053/j.sempedsurg.2014.07.002.; Burrows & Mason, 2004.

Cervical Lymphatic Malformation

Definition
A lymphatic malformation involving the neck; it may be macrocystic, microcystic, or mixed.
Causes / pathophysiology
Congenital abnormal development of lymphatic channels.
Symptoms / key features
Neck swelling; appearance and functional effects vary with size and depth.
Complications
Infection, bleeding, swallowing or airway effects, and cosmetic concerns.
Diagnosis
Clinical assessment and ultrasound or MRI.
Treatment
Observation, sclerotherapy, or surgery may be appropriate; management is individualized.
Prognosis
Many can be managed, though residual disease or recurrence is possible.
References
Perkins JA et al. “Lymphatic malformations: current cellular and clinical investigations.” Otolaryngol Head Neck Surg. 2010;142(6):789–794.; Wassef et al., 2015 (ISSVA classification).

Orbital Lymphatic Malformation

Definition
A lymphatic malformation involving tissues in or around the orbit.
Causes / pathophysiology
Congenital abnormal development of lymphatic channels.
Symptoms / key features
Intermittent or persistent eyelid and orbital swelling; the eye may protrude.
Complications
Bleeding into the lesion, pressure on the eye, or vision effects.
Diagnosis
Ophthalmic examination and MRI.
Treatment
Observation or specialist-directed sclerotherapy and surgery, depending on symptoms and anatomy.
Prognosis
Requires monitoring; recurrence and vision risk depend on extent and behavior.
References
Perkins JA et al., 2010.; Wassef et al., 2015 (ISSVA classification).
