Find a Vascular Anomalies Specialist

Vascular malformations are uncommon and may need several specialties working together. These directories and center pages can help patients find a team with relevant experience.

Good starting point: look for a multidisciplinary vascular anomalies center or clinic. The International Society for the Study of Vascular Anomalies (ISSVA) maintains a specialist and multidisciplinary team directory. Its public specialist list includes members who opted to display their information, so it may not be complete. ISSVA resources · ISSVA Find a Specialist

Specialties that may be involved

Interventional Radiology

Uses imaging to diagnose and treat lesions with procedures such as sclerotherapy for selected slow-flow malformations or embolization for selected fast-flow AVMs.

Often involved in: venous, lymphatic, and arteriovenous malformations.

Dermatology / Pediatric Dermatology

Evaluates skin findings, helps distinguish vascular anomaly types, and may provide laser or medical treatment for selected lesions.

Often involved in: capillary malformations, port-wine birthmarks, and skin lesions associated with syndromes.

Vascular Anomalies Surgery

Plastic, pediatric, general, orthopedic, or head-and-neck surgeons may be involved when a lesion affects function, causes symptoms, or needs removal or reconstruction.

Often involved in: complex lesions, overgrowth, or lesions affecting the face, airway, limbs, or organs.

Hematology / Oncology and Vascular Medicine

May help manage clotting or bleeding complications and coordinate medical therapies for selected complex vascular anomalies.

Often involved in: extensive venous or lymphatic malformations, coagulopathy, or complex syndromes.

Clinical Genetics / Genetic Counseling

Can assess whether a vascular anomaly may be part of a genetic syndrome, discuss testing, and explain implications for relatives.

Often involved in: multiple lesions, family history, overgrowth, or syndromic features.

Neurology, Neurosurgery, and Neurointerventional Specialists

Evaluate brain or spinal AVMs and neurologic complications; the appropriate team depends on the lesion’s location and symptoms.

Often involved in: cerebral or spinal AVMs and Sturge-Weber syndrome.

Other Site-Specific Specialists

Ophthalmology, otolaryngology, pulmonology, gastroenterology, cardiology, orthopedics, rehabilitation, and lymphedema therapy may contribute when the affected body system calls for their expertise.

Johns Hopkins Vascular Anomalies Center — Baltimore, Maryland

The center lists interventional radiology, plastic surgery, ENT, orthopedics, general surgery, dermatology, hematology/oncology, and genetics. Its director is Clifford Raabe Weiss, MD, an interventional radiologist.

Center and current care team

Children’s Hospital of Philadelphia Comprehensive Vascular Anomalies Program — Philadelphia, Pennsylvania

Pediatric multidisciplinary program directed by Denise Adams, MD. The current team listing includes interventional radiology, lymphatics, dermatology, genetics, neurology, neurosurgery, orthopedics, ENT, surgery, and other specialties.

Program team and contact information

Stanford Medicine Children’s Health Vascular Anomalies Team — Palo Alto, California

The team lists dermatology, hematology/oncology, radiology and interventional radiology, ENT, plastic surgery, hand and upper-limb surgery, genetics, and cardiology. Joyce Teng, MD, PhD, is listed as director of Pediatric Dermatology with vascular-anomalies expertise.

Current team and specialties

Choosing a clinic: confirm that the team treats the patient’s specific malformation and age group, ask which specialties review cases together, and check referral and insurance requirements. Provider rosters and services can change, so confirm details with the clinic.
