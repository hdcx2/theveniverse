Capillary Malformations

A guide to capillary birthmarks and syndromes that can include capillary lesions.

Type: Slow-flow capillary lesions Examples: Port-wine birthmarks and capillary malformation syndromes Model files: Add CAD/3D files to this condition when available

Conditions and presentations

Capillary Malformation–Arteriovenous Malformation (CM-AVM)

Definition
A syndrome with multiple small capillary malformations and possible fast-flow arteriovenous malformations or fistulas.
Causes / pathophysiology
Often caused by inherited variants in RASA1 or EPHB4; genetic evaluation can help guide care.
Symptoms / key features
Multiple pink or red skin lesions; some people also have deeper fast-flow lesions that cause symptoms.
Complications
Complications depend on whether and where an AVM or fistula is present; bleeding or neurologic effects can occur.
Diagnosis
Clinical and family history, imaging when indicated, and genetic testing.
Treatment
Care is individualized; options for a detected AVM may include monitoring, embolization, or surgery by a specialist team.
Prognosis
Variable; recognition of associated fast-flow lesions helps guide surveillance and care.
References
Eerola I et al. “Capillary malformation-arteriovenous malformation, a new clinical and genetic disorder caused by RASA1 mutations.” Am J Hum Genet. 2003;73(6):1240–1249. doi:10.1086/379793.; RASA1 testing and family management.

Port-Wine Birthmark (Nevus Flammeus)

Definition
A congenital, flat pink, red, or purple capillary birthmark that may darken or thicken over time.
Causes / pathophysiology
Usually sporadic and related to a localized capillary malformation.
Symptoms / key features
Persistent flat patch, often on the face or neck; appearance varies with location and age.
Complications
Some lesions may darken, thicken, or develop soft-tissue changes. Facial distribution may prompt assessment for associated conditions.
Diagnosis
Usually clinical examination; additional assessment depends on lesion location and associated findings.
Treatment
Pulsed-dye laser may be considered for color and appearance; care is individualized.
Prognosis
The birthmark generally persists; appearance and associated effects vary.
References
Chen JK et al. “An overview of clinical and experimental treatment modalities for port wine stains.” J Am Acad Dermatol. 2012;67(2):289–304. doi:10.1016/j.jaad.2011.11.938.; Wassef et al., 2015 (ISSVA classification).

Salmon Patch (Nevus Simplex)

Definition
A common, flat, faint pink capillary birthmark, often on the eyelids, forehead, or nape of the neck.
Causes / pathophysiology
A common congenital capillary birthmark.
Symptoms / key features
Usually asymptomatic; the color may become more visible with crying, straining, or warmth.
Complications
Usually none; many facial lesions fade during early childhood, while nape lesions may persist.
Diagnosis
Clinical examination.
Treatment
Usually no treatment is needed.
Prognosis
Often fades naturally, particularly on the face.

Sturge-Weber Syndrome (SWS)

Definition
A sporadic neurocutaneous condition that may include a facial capillary malformation, abnormal vessels involving the brain, and eye involvement such as glaucoma.
Causes / pathophysiology
Usually associated with a post-zygotic somatic GNAQ variant.
Symptoms / key features
May include seizures, weakness, developmental or learning differences, headaches, and eye problems; features vary.
Complications
Neurologic, ocular, and developmental complications vary by the structures involved.
Diagnosis
Clinical assessment, brain MRI and EEG when indicated, and regular ophthalmologic evaluation.
Treatment
Specialist care may address seizures, glaucoma, and skin findings; treatment depends on the person’s symptoms.
Prognosis
Variable; early recognition and management of neurologic and eye involvement are important.
References
Comi AM. “Pathophysiology of Sturge-Weber syndrome.” J Child Neurol. 2003;18(8):509–516. doi:10.1177/08830738030180080701.; Yeom & Comi. “Updates on Sturge-Weber Syndrome.” Stroke. 2022;53(12):3769–3779.

Related vascular anomalies

Klippel-Trenaunay Syndrome

A combined capillary-lymphatic-venous malformation with limb overgrowth in some cases.

Cherry Angioma

A small benign vascular papule; it is generally classified as a vascular tumor rather than a capillary malformation.

Different category

KTS is listed here as a related combined anomaly, not a simple capillary malformation. A cherry angioma is also kept separate because it belongs to a different vascular-anomaly category. See the ISSVA 2025 Classification & Glossary.
